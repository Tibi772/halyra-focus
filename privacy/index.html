Halyra Focus / Privacy

Privacy

Last updated: November 2026

Short version: Halyra Focus has no analytics, no tracking and no ads. You don’t need an account. If you choose to create one, it is used only to keep your devices in sync.

The app

  • Your block lists, schedules, settings and statistics are stored only on your computer, in the app’s own folder.
  • Website blocking writes the domains you choose into your computer’s hosts file through a local helper. Nothing is sent over the internet.
  • To block apps, the app looks at the list of running programs on your computer. This list never leaves your computer.
  • The app does not connect to the internet by itself. The only exception is the AI, and only when you use it: the text you type into Halyra AI, the style studio or the smart setup is sent to Google’s Gemini API — through Halyra’s server if you are signed in, or straight from your computer if you added your own key — to produce the answer. Nothing else is ever sent.
  • Opening a link (for example to this website) happens in your browser.
  • Daily limits count how long a list’s apps run and, on a Mac, read the address of the front browser tab (only after you allow it). This is used only to count time on your computer and is never sent anywhere.

Optional account & sync

  • If you sign in, we store your email address and a password hash (handled by our hosting provider, Supabase) and one record with your block lists, schedules, custom styles, a few preferences and the currently running focus session.
  • Database rules make sure only your signed-in account can read or change your record. It is not shared, sold or analysed.
  • Never synced: your statistics, PIN, AI key, usage times and the list of apps on your computer.
  • Delete everything by asking in the source repository’s issues, or simply stop using the account; signing out removes the login from the device.

This website

  • No cookies, no analytics, no tracking scripts, no third-party fonts.
  • The website and downloads are hosted on GitHub. GitHub may log technical data such as IP addresses when you visit or download; see GitHub’s privacy statement.
