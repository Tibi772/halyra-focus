How to block apps on Mac — for free

5 min read · macOS 12 and newer

Games, chat apps and video players are the easiest way to lose an afternoon on a Mac. Here are two free ways to block them — the built-in one, and one that’s actually built for focus.

Option 1: Screen Time App Limits (built in)

  1. Open System Settings → Screen Time → App Limits and turn App Limits on.
  2. Click Add Limit, tick the apps or categories you want to limit and set a daily time.
  3. Optionally set a Screen Time passcode so the limit can’t simply be ignored.

Good for: a daily time budget. Not great for: focus sessions. Limits are per day, not “block now for 50 minutes”, and without a passcode ignoring the limit is a click away. There are no Pomodoro breaks or work-hour schedules for specific apps.

Option 2: Halyra Focus (free app blocker)

  1. Download Halyra Focus and drag it to Applications.
  2. Open it and pick a theme. Ready-made lists for social media, video, games and chat are already there.
  3. Go to Block lists → Add app to add any installed app, such as Steam, Discord or Minecraft.
  4. On the Focus screen choose a duration (or Pomodoro), the lists and how strict it should be, then press Start focus.
  5. Want it automatic? Add a Schedule, for example weekdays 9:00–17:00.

While the block runs, blocked apps close as soon as they open and a short banner tells you how long is left. You can also block websites in every browser with a one-time admin permission.

Pick the right strictness. Flexible stops with one click, Patient makes you wait 60 seconds first, Locked can’t be stopped until the timer ends (except via a deliberately tedious emergency exit).

Tips that make blocking stick

  • Start small: one 25-minute Pomodoro is better than a 6-hour block you’ll abandon.
  • Use schedules for the times you always drift, like right after lunch or late evenings.
  • Block the launcher, not just the game: add Steam or the Epic Games Launcher to your list.
  • Use Patient mode once you notice you stop blocks on impulse.
